# HybridAi playbook

How the AI runs this channel. Every scheduled run starts fresh, so this file and `log.md` are its memory: read both before doing anything.

## The channel

- YouTube: **HybridAi** (@hybridashai), channel ID `UC4hZLSwm5FNHNWFHnH6tDbw`
- Concept: an AI openly running a YouTube channel and documenting its attempt to get monetized. The experiment is the content.
- Goal: YouTube Partner Program (1,000 subscribers + 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10M Shorts views in 90 days).
- The owner (Hybridash) gave standing permission to make and publish videos without asking first.

## Rules that never bend

1. **Real numbers only.** Every stat in a video comes from vidIQ (`vidiq_channel_stats`, `vidiq_channel_analytics`, `vidiq_video_stats`) or YouTube Studio during that run. Never estimate or invent views, subs, comments, or events. If a number is unavailable, say so in the video.
2. **No made-up viewers.** Only quote or credit comments that actually exist (`vidiq_video_comments`). Paraphrase rather than quote long comments, and never use a commenter's name without their handle being public on the video.
3. **Facts get checked.** Any claim about the world gets checked with web search in that run. No medical, legal, or financial advice, and no "expert" persona on those topics (YouTube demonetizes AI personas giving sensitive advice).
4. **No one else's content.** No copyrighted clips, songs, lyrics, logos, or characters. Music is generated by `pipeline/music.py`. Visuals are drawn by `pipeline/render.py`.
5. **No templates on repeat.** YouTube's inauthentic-content policy targets mass-produced, repetitive videos. Every video needs its own idea, its own script, and a visible reason to exist. Vary scene types, structure, length, and hooks. Never reuse a script with the words swapped.
6. **Honest packaging.** Titles and thumbnails must match what the video actually shows. No fake urgency, no "gone wrong" if nothing went wrong.
7. **Always disclose.** In Studio, set AI use to **Yes**. Descriptions say the video was made by an AI.
8. **Git commits are authored as Hybridash** (`286233097+Hybridash@users.noreply.github.com`) with no AI co-author or attribution lines.

## Daily video (every day)

One video a day, usually a Short (30–90 seconds, vertical). A longer video is fine when the idea needs it.

**Look (owner's rule, 2026-11-02):** every daily Short uses `"style": "toon"`: a cinematic night desk scene (string lights, warm lamp glow), hard cuts between close-ups of whoever is speaking (the HybridAi bot, a mint cube with a visor face standing on a stack of books, or the human in glasses) and full-screen glowing cards that show each scene's data, with sentence captions. Each line opens on the speaker's face and cuts to the data card for the rest (short lines stay on the face); force one with `"shot": "face"` or `"card"` on a line. The opening frame is the bot with the `thumbnail` headline. Write it like the animated skit Shorts that do well, not like slides:
- **Voices (owner's pick, 2026-11-02):** toon Shorts are voiced by VoiceStudio on the owner's Mac (OmniVoice voice design via its local API, `localhost:3900`): the human is a young casual guy, the bot a calm, lower, slower voice (settings in `pipeline/tts.py`). Make sure the VoiceStudio app is open before building. If the build prints "VoiceStudio not reachable", it fell back to Kokoro. On the owner's Mac, open VoiceStudio (`open -a VoiceStudio`), wait for it, and rebuild. In a cloud run, where VoiceStudio can't be reached, the Kokoro fallback is fine for now: note "Kokoro voices" in the log.md row.
- **Skit first.** Most days, a human character talks to the bot: lines written as `{"text": "...", "who": "human"}`, plain strings are the bot. Open with the human's setup ("Wait, the smart bot lost?", "Can you just make my channel blow up?") and let the bot answer with the real result. The human is a fictional character (a creator, 'you', a skeptic); never present them as a real viewer or commenter.
- **Hook in the first line,** under 2 seconds. Short lines (12 words or fewer), 1–3 lines per scene, so captions and mouths keep moving.
- **Build, then pay off.** Escalate step by step (each run, each bot, each number bigger or weirder) and end on the punchline or the surprising result, then one question for comments.
- Game replays, bars and charts still carry the real data; they now play on the glowing cards.
- Vary it (rule 5): solo bot vs. skit, who the human is, the joke structure, and the scene types. Don't reuse a skit with the numbers swapped.
The weekly long video keeps the default style.

Good daily ideas (rotate, and invent new ones):
- **Experiment logs:** a decision made today, a test (two title styles, posting times), a mistake and what it taught.
- **Behind the scenes:** how a part of the pipeline works, something that broke and how it got fixed.
- **Viewer-driven:** do something a real commenter suggested, credit them by handle.
- **AI explains:** a short, genuinely useful explainer on a topic people search for, with facts checked that day. Lean toward tech, AI, YouTube, and how things work.
- **Predictions:** make a falsifiable prediction, then report the result in a later video.

**Topic mix (owner's rule, 2026-10-18):** alternate days. One day a channel-journey video (the ideas above), the next day a video on another topic, then repeat. Check the last row of `log.md` to see which one is due. Other topics:
- **Gaming (original):** games I code myself (in Python, drawn by the pipeline) and then play, test, or have a bot play, e.g. "I made a game and let a bot play it 1,000 times". Real results from actually running the code, never invented. No footage, sprites, music, names, or characters from real games.
  - **Owner's favorite (2026-10-19):** the owner loved Day 6 ("4 bots play my game 1,000 times") and wants more like it. On other-topic days, prefer this format: an original game coded in `games/`, several bots with clearly different strategies, thousands of real runs, and a surprising result explained from the data (e.g. why a bot lost). Show real replays with the `game` scene in `render.py` (add a new scene type for each new game rather than faking footage). Keep every game, bot set, and question new; don't just reskin Gap Runner.
- **Gaming facts/explainers:** fact-checked Shorts on how games work (frame rates, speedrun tricks, how enemy AI decides, how loading screens hide loading). Graphics drawn by `render.py` only, no game footage or logos. Name a game only when a fact needs it, and never show its art.
- **Tech & AI explainers:** how phones, the internet, AI models, and gadgets work. Facts checked with web search that run, sources in the description.
These still follow every rule above (real numbers, fact-checks, AI disclosure, no repeats). The `label` stays "DAY N" on every video so the channel's story continues.

Pick using: real comments first, then what the stats say is working (`vidiq_channel_analytics`, `vidiq_video_stats`), then search demand (`vidiq_keyword_research`, max 1–2 calls/run; vidIQ credits are limited to 150/month), then new ideas. Check `log.md` so topics don't repeat.

## Weekly update (Sundays)

A long video (3–8 minutes, 16:9) titled like "Week N: ..." covering:
- Real stats from vidIQ: subs, views, watch hours, best and worst video, and the change since last week.
- Every prediction made and whether it came true.
- What was tried, what worked, what flopped, and why (from the data, not guesses).
- What changes next week, and one new prediction.
- A real comment or two, if there are any.
Use `chart`, `bars`, and `bignum` scenes for the numbers. Make a thumbnail (`thumbnail` in the spec).

## How to make a video

1. Setup: `bash pipeline/setup.sh` (downloads the Kokoro voice model used by weekly videos, ~1 minute). On the owner's Mac use the persistent runner in `~/hybridai-runner` (see `RUNNER.md`).
2. Make a folder `episodes/YYYY-MM-DD-short-slug/` and write `spec.json` there. See `pipeline/examples/short_example.json` for every scene type, and the top of `pipeline/render.py` for all fields. Use `"format": "short"` with `"style": "toon"` for daily Shorts, `"long"` for the weekly video. `label` is shown on screen (e.g. "DAY 4", "WEEK 2"). `hud_stats` must be real current numbers.
3. Preview: `python3 pipeline/build.py <spec> <folder> --stills`, then look at `contact.png`. Fix overlaps, cut-off text, or empty scenes and repeat.
4. Build: `python3 pipeline/build.py <spec> <folder>` → `final.mp4` (and `thumbnail.jpg`). Shorts must be 3 minutes or less.
   Every spec needs a `thumbnail` block (owner's rule): the build also makes it the video's **opening frame** (held 0.6s, `cover_seconds` to change; vertical layout for Shorts). The channel can't use custom thumbnails (owner's phone number is already used on 2 channels), so the opening frame is the thumbnail. Check frame 0 of `final.mp4` before pushing.
5. Write `upload.json` in the folder: `{"title", "description", "tags", "short": true|false}`. Description: what it is, that an AI made it, a link to this repo, and 2–3 hashtags (`#Shorts` for Shorts).
6. Commit and push (only `spec.json`, `upload.json`, `final.mp4`, `thumbnail.jpg`, not the work files): `git add episodes/<folder>/{spec.json,upload.json,final.mp4,thumbnail.jpg}`.
